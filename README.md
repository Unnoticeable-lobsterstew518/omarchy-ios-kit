omarchy-ios-kit is a kit for iOS development on Linux (Omarchy). It is built around a single, ambitious idea: build, run, debug, and hot-reload iPhone apps without a Mac.

Traditionally, making an iPhone app meant buying Apple hardware, installing a large development suite, and staying locked inside that ecosystem. omarchy-ios-kit takes a different path. It gathers the tools, scripts, and configuration needed to turn an ordinary Linux setup into a working iOS development environment, then wraps the whole thing in a workflow that is easy to follow.

The kit is designed for people who want to see their app on a phone screen quickly. You make a change to your code, save it, and the change appears on the running app almost instantly through hot-reload. You can pause the app, inspect what is happening, and fix problems as they appear. The whole loop of write, run, watch, and repair happens in one place.

Even if you have never built a mobile app before, the name of the kit tells you what it is for: it is a starting point, a set of building blocks, and a guided path rather than a blank page.

## 💡 Tips for a Smooth First Day

Start with the example project rather than your own app. Seeing a known-good app build, run, and hot-reload proves that the whole chain works. Only after that should you point the kit at your own code, because then any problem you hit is clearly about your project and not about the environment.

Keep one small window open for the app while you edit in another. The pleasure of this workflow is watching the two stay in sync.

Save often. Hot-reload rewards small, frequent saves far more than large, rare ones, and small saves make it obvious which change caused a new behavior.

Do not skip the debugging step just because things are working. Open the debugging view once while the app is healthy, so you already know what it looks like when you need it in a hurry.
